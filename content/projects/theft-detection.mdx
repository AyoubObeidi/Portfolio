## Problem

Shoplifting in shops and supermarkets often goes unnoticed because nobody can
watch every camera feed all the time. Theft Detection watches the feeds instead.
It flags shoplifting, loitering, intrusions into restricted areas and fighting,
then sounds an alarm and sends an alert.

## Process

**Several cameras at once.** Each camera, whether a local webcam or an RTSP
network camera, reads frames on its own thread. One slow feed doesn't hold up
the others or freeze the dashboard. People are tracked per camera, keyed by
camera and track ID, so IDs from different feeds never clash.

**Behaviour, not just objects.** YOLOv8 detects people and items, and a YOLOv8
pose model tracks their posture. Together they catch:

- a hand moving from a picked-up item to a pocket or bag
- someone staying in a zone longer than a set threshold
- a wrist crossing into a restricted area, such as behind the till
- sudden bending in low-visibility aisles

**Zones drawn on the live feed.** Operators draw security zones as polygons over
the live video in the dashboard. The coordinates are mapped to a fixed 1280×720
grid, so a zone stays in the same place whatever screen it was drawn on.

**Alerts that always play.** The siren is synthesised in the browser with the
Web Audio API instead of loading an audio file, with a 3-second cooldown to
avoid alarm fatigue. Alerts and snapshots also go out by email and Telegram.

**Optional face recognition.** Faces can be registered on a blacklist, which
raises a high-priority alarm, or a whitelist for staff and regular customers.
If the face recognition library isn't installed, that panel turns off and
everything else keeps working.

## Outcome

- A FastAPI backend that streams detections over WebSockets to a Next.js
  dashboard.
- The dashboard shows live feeds, a heatmap of activity per camera, CPU and
  memory use, weekly alert trends, and a searchable alert history that exports
  to CSV.
