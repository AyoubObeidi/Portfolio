## Problem

Video courses are hard to search. When a learner wants to revisit one idea,
they have to remember which lesson covered it and scrub through the video to
find the moment.

Vertex lets a learner type a question in plain language and get back ranked
cards. Each card links to the exact second in a lesson's video where that topic
is taught, and the video plays on the site.

## My role

I built Vertex on my own: the Sanity content model and Studio, the Next.js
site, the search pipeline, the video ingestion scripts and the Vercel
deployment. Every change went through a pull request with automated review
from CodeRabbit.

## Constraints

- **Time.** The first full version, from the first commit to the deployed
  site, took five days.
- **A function time limit.** Search runs several model calls in a row, and
  all of them have to finish inside one serverless function's time limit.
- **A free model.** Search first ran on a free-tier model, which was slow.
- **Secrets stay on the server.** The browser only ever posts to
  `/api/search`. It never sees the model key, the search endpoint or the
  Sanity tokens.

## Process

Authors create and manage courses in Sanity, and a Next.js site serves them to
learners. Search is built with the AI SDK, and Clerk handles sign-in.

**Search writes its own queries.** `/api/search` connects to Sanity's Context
MCP server, gives the model the deployed schema and a system prompt, and lets
it write GROQ queries over the catalog. After a few round trips, a final step
returns structured results. Zod validates them, so only results grounded in
what the queries returned reach the page.

**Transcripts are prepared ahead of time.** Offline scripts fetch each video's
captions with yt-dlp and store the transcript as short timestamped chunks. A
hand-written chapter list gives every video a table of contents, so search
matches chapters first and falls back to the noisier transcript only when it
has to. Whole transcripts never reach the model.

**Results open at the right second.** A video result links to the lesson page
with a start time, and the player passes it to the video provider's embed. The
video plays on the site, starting at that moment.

**Progress is saved for each learner.** When a lesson's video ends, the lesson
is marked complete. A My Learning page lists started courses and resumes each
one at the last second watched.

## Outcome

Live at [vertex-iota-ashy.vercel.app](https://vertex-iota-ashy.vercel.app).

- Ten sample courses in Sanity, with course, lesson, search and My Learning
  pages.
- Search now runs on a Gemini model, after the free-tier model proved too slow
  for its chain of calls.

## What I'd do differently

I'd test the model's speed before building search around it. The free-tier
model took about 13 seconds for a trivial reply, and search makes several
calls, so it timed out both locally and in production.

I'd also set the function's time limit from a measured slow query, not a
guess. My first limit was 60 seconds, below search's own 90-second deadline.
The platform cut slow searches off first, and learners got a bare error the
search page couldn't show.
